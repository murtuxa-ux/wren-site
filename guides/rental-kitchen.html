7 Rental Kitchen Upgrades Under $25

By Wren HaleUpdated 25 Sep 20267 itemsall under $25

My kitchen came with brass hardware from a decade I wasn't born in and a single overhead light. Every fix here screws into nothing, drills into nothing, and cost me less than a takeout order. I've redone this same kitchen twice as I've moved, so I know what survives a second install.

1. Peel-and-stick cabinet handles, matte black

This is the Monday reel fix, zero holes. Wipe every cabinet door with rubbing alcohol first, because grease and old cleaner residue are the reason these fall off after a week, not the adhesive itself. Press each handle for a full 30 seconds, then leave the doors closed overnight before you touch them. A hairdryer on low loosens the glue at move-out.

2. Rechargeable under-cabinet puck lights

Three pucks stick under the upper cabinets with 3M tape and turn on with a tap, no wiring and no outlet needed. Mine run about three weeks per charge with daily use, then it's a USB cable overnight. This is the single fix that made my counter stop feeling like a cave at night.

Buy the alcohol wipes even if the listing doesn't push them. Every adhesive fix in this kitchen lasted longer once I started cleaning the surface first, and skipping it is the most common reason these come loose early.

3. Motion-sensor LED strip

Runs along the underside of the counter lip and switches on when you walk into the kitchen at 2 a.m., which matters more than it sounds like it should. Adhesive backing, USB rechargeable, and it peels off clean since it's stuck to the underside of the cabinet, not a visible surface. Cut it to length at the marked notches only, cutting elsewhere kills that section of the strip.

4. Matte black faucet sprayer attachment

Screws onto the end of a standard kitchen faucet by hand, no tools, no plumber. Unscrew the little aerator cap at the tip first and check the threads match before you order, most standard faucets do. Takes about two minutes and changes how the whole sink reads, this was the Tuesday reel swap.

5. Adhesive shelf liner

Lines cabinet shelves and drawers so you're not looking at bare particleboard every time you open a door, and it protects the original shelf finish, which matters for your deposit. Wipe shelves clean and dry first, then lay it flat and smooth from one edge rather than trying to reposition once it's down. Comes up in one sheet without leaving glue behind.

6. Magnetic knife strip, adhesive mount

Mounts with the same 3M-style adhesive strips as the handles, so it needs a clean, dry, flat section of wall or the underside of a cabinet. Rated for kitchen knives up to a certain weight per strip, listed on the package, don't hang your heaviest cast iron pan from it. Frees up a whole drawer, which in a 650 sq ft kitchen is not nothing.

7. Peel-and-stick backsplash tile

Covers a dated or damaged backsplash strip above the counter without touching what's underneath. Dry the wall fully and work from one end down a row rather than jumping around, that's how you keep the grout lines lining up. Give it a wipe with a damp cloth, not a scrub, once it's up, harsh cleaners can lift the edges early.

Take a photo of every original fixture before you start. If a handle strips paint off a cabinet door on removal, you want proof of what it looked like going in.

Move-out day. Everything above comes off with heat and patience. Warm the adhesive with a hairdryer on low, peel slow at a low angle instead of pulling straight out, and clean any leftover residue with a little rubbing alcohol on a soft cloth. Keep the original hardware in a labeled bag in a drawer so it goes back on in the same order it came off.
